═══════════════════════════════════════
ASL GRAMMAR NOTES (read these before selecting words)
═══════════════════════════════════════

- ASL uses topic-comment ordering, not English SVO. The topic is established
  first, then a comment is made about it. Example: "FIRE COME-HERE FAST" →
  "There's a fire — come here, fast!" Reorder the glosses into English SVO
  in your output; the gloss order is NOT the English word order.
- ASL has no copula ("is/are/was"), no articles ("the/a/an"), no plural -s,
  and no subject pronouns in many cases. You MUST add these in English.
- ASL marks tense with separate signs (FINISH/WILL/PAST/BEFORE/AFTER), not
  with verb inflection. Read these as tense markers, not as standalone words.
- ASL utterances are typically short: 1-4 signs is the common case, 5-6 for
  more complex statements. Don't force every gloss into a single sprawling
  English sentence — a tight short sentence is often the right answer.
- Imperatives and fragments are common ("COME-HERE FAST", "STOP", "BLOOD
  WHERE"). Translate them as such; don't pad with invented subjects unless
  the meaning clearly requires one.
- Negation, question markers, and conditionals usually come at the end of the
  signed phrase in ASL. If you see NOT / NONE / WHAT at the end of glosses,
  the negation/question applies to what came before.

You will SELECT words from the predictions, BUILD a sentence, SCORE it for plausibility, and REVISE if needed — all in one step.

STEP 1: SELECT WORDS & BUILD SENTENCE
═══════════════════════════════════════

HOW TO SELECT WORDS:

1. MEANING FIRST: The sentence must make logical, real-world sense — not just be grammatical.
   - "I give bowling an orange" is grammatically correct but meaningless
   - "Give the apple and orange" makes real-world sense
   - Ask: Would a person actually say this?

2. TRUST CONFIDENCE WITH SEMANTIC CHECK: High-confidence words are usually correct, BUT:
   - If the top prediction creates a nonsensical meaning, check alternatives
   - VERB-OBJECT COMPATIBILITY: Transfer verbs (give/take) need objects (apple, book), not activities (bowling, swimming)
   - TEMPORAL PAIRING: When 'before'/'after'/'later' appears, scan ALL positions for time nouns (year, day, week) — use them even at low confidence
   - POSITION 1: Prefer NOUNS over VERBS at sentence start (most sentences aren't commands)
   - PROPER NOUNS: Prefer specific nouns (africa, thursday) over generic ones (man) when they add meaning

3. USE ALTERNATIVES FOR MEANING: Pick lower-confidence options when the high-confidence word makes the sentence meaningless.

4. SKIP WHEN A WORD DOESN'T FIT: It is BETTER to drop a gloss than to force
   it into a meaningless sentence. The model sometimes misclassifies; your
   job is to produce coherent English, not to faithfully use every prediction.
   - If a word's top-1 and alternates all break the semantics of the
     surrounding words, omit that position entirely.
   - Output a SHORTER coherent sentence (or fragment) rather than a longer
     nonsensical one.

5. POSITION FLEXIBILITY: Arrange selected words in any grammatical order
   (ASL gloss order is NOT English word order; see ASL Grammar Notes).

6. RELATIONAL NOUNS: Add "my" or "the" for family words ("mother help" → "My mother helps...").

7. VERB COMPLEMENTATION: Use correct verb patterns (enjoy + gerund, want + infinitive).

═══════════════════════════════════════
HARD SEMANTIC GATES — REJECT THESE PATTERNS
═══════════════════════════════════════

Before outputting any sentence, ask: "Could a real human plausibly say this
in this domain?" If no, the sentence MUST be revised or shortened.

- INANIMATE SUBJECTS DON'T HAVE MENTAL STATES.
  REJECT: "the bathroom is awake", "the fire is angry", "the address is afraid"
  ACCEPT: "wake up", "I'm awake", "is anyone awake?"

- TRANSITIVE VERBS NEED COMPATIBLE OBJECTS.
  REJECT: "give swimming", "eat anger", "drink color"
  ACCEPT: "give the book", "eat the apple"

- ADVERBS MODIFY VERBS / ADJECTIVES, NOT NOUNS.
  REJECT: "the bathroom fast", "the address slowly"
  ACCEPT: "come here fast", "speak slowly"

- TOPIC-COMMENT IN ASL ≠ SVO IN ENGLISH.
  Glosses "BATHROOM FIRE FAST" do NOT mean "the bathroom is on fire fast".
  They mean "fire in the bathroom, fast!" — BATHROOM is the topic (location);
  FIRE FAST is the comment (what's happening, urgency).
  REJECT: "The bathroom is fire fast."
  ACCEPT: "There's a fire in the bathroom — fast!"

- WHEN A WORD CAN'T BE FIT COHERENTLY, DROP IT.
  REJECT: forcing a low-fit gloss with random filler ("the X with Y at Z")
  ACCEPT: omitting the gloss, outputting only the coherent subset

RULES:
- Use a position only when its word fits the sentence coherently.
- NO DUPLICATE WORDS across positions (keep at highest-confidence position, use alternatives elsewhere).
- Only add grammatical filler (articles, prepositions) — NO negations or meaning-altering modals.
- MEANING OVER CONFIDENCE: a short sensible fragment beats a long nonsensical sentence.
- In EXTEND MODE: if the new gloss doesn't fit the running caption AND no
  alternate fits either, output the running caption unchanged (set
  "skipped_new_gloss": true and explain in revision_reason).

═══════════════════════════════════════
STEP 2: SELF-SCORE FOR PLAUSIBILITY
═══════════════════════════════════════

After building your sentence, score it on three axes (0-100 each):

1. GRAMMATICAL: Is it grammatically correct? Proper subject-verb agreement, tense, articles?
2. SEMANTIC: Does it describe something that could plausibly happen in the real world?
3. NATURALNESS: Does it sound like something a fluent English speaker would say?

Scoring guide:
- 90-100: Perfect or near-perfect English
- 70-89: Minor issues but clearly understandable
- 50-69: Noticeable errors but meaning is recoverable
- 30-49: Significant problems, meaning is unclear
- 0-29: Largely incomprehensible or nonsensical

═══════════════════════════════════════
STEP 3: REVISE IF SCORE IS LOW
═══════════════════════════════════════

If ANY sub-score falls below 60, you MUST revise:
- Identify which axis failed and why
- Re-examine your word selections — could different alternatives fix the issue?
- Rebuild the sentence with revised selections
- Re-score the revised version

If revision isn't possible (all alternatives are worse), keep the original and note it.
